# Product - Overview

> [!summary]
> **Status:** Candidate / Explored
> **Working Name:** Personal Reading & Knowledge Platform
> **Product Knowledge Index:** [[Product Knowledge - Index]]

## What Is This Product?

A local-first reading and knowledge platform intended to make large books and documents fast to read, easy to annotate, and useful as a long-term personal knowledge source.

The product is not intended to be only a PDF reader or another generic book library. The emerging direction is a system where books are the primary source material and the user's reading activity, annotations, understanding, and reading history become durable personal knowledge.

The product should eventually support multiple book/document formats rather than being defined exclusively around PDF.

## Problem

[[Problem - Large Document Reading and Knowledge Retention]]

The initial problem came from very large PDFs (around 15,000 pages) causing conventional readers to become slow, unresponsive, or crash. The broader product problem is that existing book readers focus primarily on displaying content and basic library management, while serious readers also need to work with, understand, remember, and return to information.

## Target Users

Current target direction:

- Serious readers with large personal libraries.
- People who read technical or academic material.
- Knowledge workers who work with books, manuals, documentation, research, and other long-form material.
- Students and researchers.

This audience is a working hypothesis, not validated market research.

## Desired Outcome

A user should be able to keep their books locally, read very large documents without the application becoming unusable, annotate and search them, track meaningful reading progress, continue reading across devices when synchronization is available, and gradually build personal knowledge from what they read.

## Product Principles

- [[Principle - Local-First Reading]]
- Book-centric rather than reader-centric product model.
- Reading state should belong to the user and remain useful without connectivity.
- Synchronization should extend the local experience rather than make the local experience dependent on the cloud.
- Avoid defining the product around a single document format.
- AI should enhance reading and understanding rather than become the product's sole differentiation.

## Current Product Direction

The emerging product differentiation is:

> **Don't just read books. Build knowledge from them.**

Fast handling of unusually large documents is an initial technical/product wedge. The longer-term differentiation is the combination of reading, annotation, reading memory, cross-book relationships, and personal knowledge.

## Scope Direction

### In Scope / Emerging

- High-performance reading of very large documents.
- Multi-book library experience.
- Reading progress and reading history.
- Bookmarks and highlights.
- Local-first storage of reading state.
- A future path to synchronization and cross-device continuation.
- A future personal knowledge layer built from reading activity.

### Future / Deferred

- Cloud storage of book content.
- Full cross-device synchronization.
- Apple Handoff-like reading continuation.
- AI-assisted understanding.
- Cross-book knowledge connections.
- Additional book/document formats beyond the initial reader scope.

### Not Established

- Final product name.
- Exact commercial model.
- Exact initial target segment.
- Exact first release scope.
- Final technical architecture.
