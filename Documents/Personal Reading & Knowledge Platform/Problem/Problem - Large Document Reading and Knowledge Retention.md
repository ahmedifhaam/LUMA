# Problem - Large Document Reading and Knowledge Retention

> [!summary]
> **Status:** Explored
> **Product:** [[Product - Overview]]

## Problem

Existing readers can become very slow, unresponsive, or crash when opening extremely large PDFs. The concrete example discussed was a PDF of approximately 15,000 pages.

At the same time, a product aimed only at solving large-PDF rendering would face a crowded market of general book and document readers. The broader problem worth solving is the experience of people who read substantial amounts of information and need to work with it beyond simply turning pages.

## Target User

Current working direction:

- Serious readers.
- Technical and academic readers.
- Knowledge workers dealing with long-form reference material.
- Students and researchers.

This is not yet validated market segmentation.

## Context

Users may have very large PDFs or collections of books and documents. They may read across desktop and mobile devices and want to retain progress, annotations, and understanding over time.

## Pain / Friction

Observed or directly discussed friction includes:

- Very large PDFs can overwhelm conventional readers.
- Reader performance may degrade as document size increases.
- Basic book libraries do not by themselves solve the problem of understanding and retaining information.
- Continuing reading across devices is desirable.
- Cloud dependence is undesirable for the core reading experience.

## Desired Outcome

The user should be able to open and work with unusually large books/documents without the application becoming unusable, while retaining a persistent relationship with the material through progress, bookmarks, highlights, notes, and eventually personal knowledge connections.

## Current Alternatives

General-purpose PDF readers and ebook/book-library applications were considered implicitly as the existing alternative set.

## Why Existing Alternatives Are Insufficient

The initial observed gap is performance with very large PDFs. The strategic product gap is broader: existing alternatives do not necessarily combine high-performance large-document reading, local ownership of reading state, cross-device continuity, and a long-term knowledge layer in the proposed way.

This broader differentiation remains a hypothesis and requires validation.

## Evidence / Observations

- Direct user experience: a roughly 15,000-page PDF causes many readers to crash or become very sluggish.
- Product discussion established that a generic book library is not sufficient differentiation because many products already exist in that category.

## Uncertainty

The strongest initial customer segment and willingness to pay are not established.
